# Ecosystem Triangle

## Strategic View

Score Energy is not only a user-facing application. Its long-term direction is a triangular ecosystem with three participant groups:

- users
- energy consultants and professionals
- companies and solution providers

The platform's role is to mediate trust, context, and action across these groups.

## The Three Sides of the Triangle

## Users

Users are the starting point of the ecosystem.

They bring:

- energy bills and consumption context
- demand for understanding
- demand for guidance
- demand for practical solutions

Their primary goal is to reduce confusion, improve decisions, and act with confidence.

## Energy Consultants and Professionals

Consultants and professionals contribute expert interpretation and advisory support.

They become relevant when:

- the user's case is more complex than automated guidance can cover
- the user needs local or specialized help
- the product identifies situations that require human expertise

Their role is not to replace the product. Their role is to extend it where human judgment matters.

## Companies and Solution Providers

Companies and providers contribute concrete products and services such as efficiency upgrades, equipment, or implementation capacity.

They become relevant when:

- the user is ready to move from insight to solution
- a consultant recommendation needs an execution path
- the platform can match demand with relevant offerings

Their role is not just exposure. Their value depends on relevance, fit, and timing.

## Score Energy as Mediator

Score Energy should mediate the ecosystem in a structured way.

Its responsibilities include:

- translating user energy data into actionable context
- deciding when software guidance is enough
- identifying when expert help is appropriate
- surfacing relevant providers only when there is a clear use case
- maintaining trust between all sides

This mediator role is strategically important because raw marketplaces often fail when they expose users to too many choices too early.

## Why the Triangle Matters

The triangle creates a stronger product model than a standalone dashboard because it allows Score Energy to evolve from:

- understanding
- to guidance
- to action
- to ecosystem coordination

This matters because the product's long-term value is not only in showing insights. It is in helping users move from insight to outcome with the right level of support.

## Evolution Path

The product should evolve in stages.

### Stage 1: Guided user product

Focus on:

- onboarding
- invoice analysis
- mascot guidance
- score and progression
- practical next actions

### Stage 2: Assisted decision layer

Add:

- better matching logic for recommendations
- criteria for when human support should be introduced
- structured referral paths

### Stage 3: Ecosystem platform

Expand into:

- consultant participation models
- provider participation models
- trust and quality controls
- clearer marketplace and orchestration mechanics

## Strategic Constraint

The ecosystem should not be built as a generic lead marketplace.

The platform should only connect participants when:

- the user's context is understood
- the need is clear
- the timing is appropriate
- the match can preserve user trust

This keeps the ecosystem product-led instead of sales-led.
